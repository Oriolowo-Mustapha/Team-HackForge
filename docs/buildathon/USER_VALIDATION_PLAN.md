# FarmHand lightweight user-validation plan

Status: **plan only; no interviews, testimonials, endorsements or adoption results are claimed.** A human team member can run this before submission using the isolated demo and synthetic cases. Obtain permission to take notes or record; anonymize shared findings and let participants decline.

## Participants and session

Aim to recruit five livestock/poultry farmers with varied phone/connectivity and language preferences, and at least one qualified veterinarian for workflow/safety feedback. This small convenience sample is exploratory and cannot establish clinical effectiveness or market size. Record how participants were recruited and any missing groups.

Allow 20–30 minutes per participant: introduction/consent, short interview, three observed tasks and debrief. Ask participants to think aloud. Avoid coaching except when needed to stop distress or prevent confusion about real animal-health advice. Use fictional observations; do not ask participants to upload real identifying records or pay money.

## Five farmer interview questions

1. Tell me about the last time you noticed an animal or flock was unwell. What did you do first, and how did you decide whom to contact?
2. What details did a veterinarian ask for, and where did you record the signs, deaths, timing and earlier actions?
3. What made getting advice or arranging a visit difficult, including time, travel, connectivity, trust or cost?
4. On your usual phone, would you prefer typing, a photo or a voice note, and in which language? What makes that choice easier?
5. After professional advice, how do you remember instructions and report progress? What would make this workflow useful enough to return to, and how would you assess the displayed consultation fee?

Avoid asking “Would you use our great app?” Seek concrete examples and reasons. Do not imply that a participant's positive reaction proves willingness to pay.

## Three usability tasks

| Task | Moderator prompt | Observe |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Create and review a report | “Using this fictional case, choose the correct farm/flock, record five deaths and breathing difficulty, and check the information before saving.” | Subject selection, numeric accuracy, review/correction, duplicate attempts, assistance needed |
| 2. Interpret urgency and seek a vet | “Explain what this guidance means in your own words, then show how you would ask a veterinarian for help and find the fee.” | Diagnosis versus preliminary guidance, urgency comprehension, price/payment timing, trust and dangerous misunderstandings |
| 3. Follow professional feedback | “Find the veterinarian's sample plan, mark the recording step complete and add a fictional follow-up update.” | Navigation, plan comprehension, timeline/status interpretation, confidence that the update persisted |

For the veterinarian, review one synthetic incoming case and ask them to accept, inspect its history and create a non-prescribing sample follow-up plan. Ask which context is missing, which guidance could mislead, and how professional identity/availability should be verified. Record this as review feedback, not endorsement.

## Success metrics

These are **proposed targets**, not measured outcomes:

- At least 4 of 5 farmers complete each primary task without moderator intervention.
- At least 4 of 5 accurately explain that urgency guidance is preliminary and a veterinarian remains necessary.
- No observed critical misunderstanding encourages delay of urgent veterinary help or treatment without professional advice.
- Record median task time, number of assisted steps, validation errors, navigation failures and data-loss incidents; establish a baseline before setting speed targets.
- Record preferred language/channel, connectivity constraints and fee reactions as qualitative findings with sample sizes, not market estimates.

If a safety misunderstanding appears, stop and clarify. Treat it as a release issue even when task completion is otherwise high. Native-speaker and qualified-vet review are separate from general usability testing and still required for clinical/language quality claims.
